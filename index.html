Track record

Before publishing, the same model was run as of the eve of the 2015, 2019 and 2023 tournaments, using only results and rankings available then, with parameters fixed in advance. Across 141 matches the blend had the lowest log loss (0.338, vs 0.344 for our ratings alone and 0.351 for rankings alone), so it is the published version. It did not win everything: a simple World Rugby rankings model scored better on whole-tournament outcomes in 2015 and 2019, and bookmakers gave the 2019 champion (South Africa) a higher chance.

Method

Team strength blends two estimates with equal weight: our own ratings, built from every men's international since 2000 (recent matches count more, and teams with few matches are pulled toward average), and World Rugby's official ranking points converted to an expected margin. Match margins are simulated with random variation, then pools are settled with the official bonus-point and tiebreak rules, the best third-placed teams are slotted into the bracket, and knockouts are played out. Australia gets a home advantage of 4.4 points. Results are through 31 Oct 2026; World Rugby rankings of 3 Nov 2026. Inputs are official World Rugby data only.
